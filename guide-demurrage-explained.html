How Demurrage Is Calculated, Day by Day

Why a shipment left sitting for two extra days can cost far more than double.

Demurrage is the charge that grows the longer your cargo stays uncollected. Importers are often surprised by how quickly it adds up — and the reason is that demurrage isn't linear. It's built from two things working together: which day-bracket your shipment has reached, and how much it weighs.

Step 1: Count the days correctly

The day count starts on the arrival date and runs through to the date the cargo is actually gated out of the warehouse — both days are usually counted as part of the storage period, not just the gap between them. A shipment that arrives on the 1st and leaves on the 5th has been in storage for 5 days, not 4.

Step 2: Find the applicable day-bracket

Tariffs group days into brackets, and each bracket has its own per-kg (or flat) rate. A typical structure looks like:

  • Day 1–3: free or lowest rate
  • Day 3–5: next rate tier
  • Day 6–10: higher rate tier
  • Day 11 onward: highest rate tier, sometimes calculated differently

The rate that applies is generally the rate for the bracket your current day count falls into — which is why storage cost doesn't rise smoothly; it jumps at each bracket boundary.

Step 3: Apply it to weight

For shipments over roughly 50 kg, the per-day rate is multiplied by the total weight and by the number of chargeable days (days beyond the free window). This is where demurrage becomes expensive fast: a 200 kg shipment sitting for 10 days at a mid-tier rate of, say, Rs. 62/kg/day and 7 chargeable days works out to over Rs. 86,000 — before tax, D/O fee, documentation or handling are even added.

Why it feels suddenBecause rates jump at bracket boundaries rather than rising gradually, the cost of staying "just one more day" past a bracket line can be far higher than the cost of any single day inside the previous bracket. Clearing cargo a day or two before a bracket changes is often worth actively planning for.

General cargo vs. special cargo

General cargo typically gets a short free window (commonly the first 3 days) before demurrage starts. Dangerous goods and cold-storage cargo (2–8°C, 15–25°C, and freezer) are usually billed from day one, with no free period, because of the additional monitoring, equipment and safety requirements involved in storing them.

Demurrage step by step: the formula behind the number

Demurrage sounds mysterious, but for the tariffs on this site it follows a simple recipe. First find out how many days the cargo has been in the godown. Subtract the free days, if the category has any. That leaves the chargeable days. Then pick the daily rate that matches the bracket you are in and the weight of the shipment, and multiply.

For cargo of 50 kg or less: rent = flat daily rate × chargeable days. For cargo above 50 kg: rent = rate per kg × chargeable days × weight. The key detail is that the rate you use is the rate for the final day, applied to all chargeable days. It is not a running total where early days keep their cheaper rate. This is why the bill jumps when you cross from one bracket into the next.

Example 1: a light shipment crossing into the top bracket

A 30 kg general cargo shipment, gate-out on day 10 versus day 11:

Gate-outDaily rateChargeable daysRent
Day 10Rs. 1,8397Rs. 12,873
Day 11Rs. 2,1378Rs. 17,096

One extra day cost Rs. 4,223 in rent, before tax, because both the rate and the day count moved together. Staying one day less would have saved that jump.

Example 2: a large shipment of 500 kg

This is where demurrage hurts. A 500 kg consignment of general cargo is billed per kilogram, per day, and handling alone is 500 × Rs. 62 = Rs. 31,000.

Gate-outRate / kg / dayChargeable daysRentTotal incl. tax
Day 4Rs. 311Rs. 15,500Rs. 66,187
Day 10Rs. 627Rs. 217,000Rs. 297,912

Six extra days turned a bill of about Rs. 66,000 into nearly Rs. 298,000. For heavy cargo, every single day in the godown has a real price, and the cost of delay is far higher than most importers expect.

Free days versus chargeable days

General cargo in our Gerry'sDnata tariff gets three free days. Dangerous goods and cold storage categories do not; they are chargeable from day one. This is why a 40 kg DG shipment cleared in only two days still pays Rs. 5,014 in rent, while the same weight of general cargo pays nothing.

Demurrage is not the same as detention

People often mix the terms up. In shipping, demurrage is the charge for cargo or containers occupying port or terminal space past the free time, while detention is the charge for keeping a carrier's container outside the terminal. In air cargo, the warehouse charge is usually called godown rent or storage, and most people simply call the whole bill "demurrage". On this site the two words refer to the same thing: the daily storage charge that grows with time and weight.

Frequently asked questions

When does the demurrage clock start?

It starts when the cargo is received into the warehouse after the flight lands. The free days, if any, are counted from that day.

Does demurrage stop once customs releases the goods?

Not automatically. Charges continue until the cargo physically leaves the godown. If customs clears your goods on Friday evening but you collect on Monday, the weekend days still count.

Why does my rate jump at day 6 and day 11?

Tariffs use brackets. Each bracket has a higher rate, and the rate for the bracket you finish in applies to all chargeable days.

Can demurrage be waived?

Occasionally operators grant relief where the delay was caused by the operator, a documented system outage or an official holiday. It is discretionary, so keep written proof and ask early.

Is sales tax charged on demurrage too?

In our calculator the 15% sales tax applies to the whole subtotal, which includes demurrage, handling, the D/O fee and documentation.
