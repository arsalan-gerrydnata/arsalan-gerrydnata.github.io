Cold Storage Cargo: Storage Rules & Charges

Why temperature-controlled shipments cost more to store, and how the tiers differ.

Temperature-sensitive shipments — pharmaceuticals, perishable food, biological samples, chemicals — can't sit in a standard warehouse bay. They need refrigerated or frozen storage, continuous temperature monitoring, and backup power in case of an outage. All of that infrastructure costs more to run, which is reflected directly in the tariff.

The three cold-chain categories

  • 2–8°C (chilled/pharma cold chain) — for goods like vaccines, certain medicines, and perishables that need refrigeration but not freezing.
  • 15–25°C (controlled room temperature) — for goods sensitive to heat and humidity swings but that don't need refrigeration, common for some pharmaceuticals and cosmetics.
  • Freezer storage — for goods that must stay frozen, such as certain biological or food products. This is the most expensive tier, reflecting the higher energy and equipment cost of sub-zero storage.

No free storage window

Unlike general cargo, cold storage cargo is typically billed from day one. There's no grace period, because the warehouse is committing refrigeration or freezer capacity to your shipment the moment it's offloaded, whether it stays for one day or ten.

Rates scale sharply with time

Because cold storage capacity is more limited than general dry storage, the per-day rate for cold-chain cargo tends to rise more steeply across day-brackets than general cargo. A shipment left for 10 days in freezer storage can cost several times what the same weight would cost in general cargo storage for the same period.

Why speed matters most hereFor cold-chain shipments, the financial case for clearing cargo quickly is even stronger than for general cargo — both because there's no free window and because the per-day-per-kg rate is significantly higher across every bracket.

Plan clearance before arrival

Because cold storage charges compound so quickly, experienced importers of temperature-sensitive goods pre-clear as much documentation as possible — duty assessment, permits, inspection bookings — before the shipment even lands, so the cargo can be gated out within the first day or two.
