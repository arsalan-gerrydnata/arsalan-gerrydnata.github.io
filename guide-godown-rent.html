Godown Rent on Import Shipments: The Complete Guide

What godown rent actually is, why it's charged, and how the free-storage window works.

When a shipment lands at an airport or port, it doesn't clear customs and leave the same hour. Between the flight landing and the importer's truck driving out with the goods, the cargo sits in a bonded warehouse — commonly called a godown — operated by the airline's ground handling agent or a private warehousing company. Godown rent is the fee charged for that storage space, and it's calculated per day, per kilogram, based on published tariff tables.

Why does godown rent exist?

Warehouse space at an airport is limited and expensive to maintain — it needs security, climate control in many cases, forklifts, staff, and insurance. Ground handling companies recover this cost by charging importers rent for every day their cargo occupies a slot, starting from the day it's offloaded from the aircraft.

The free-storage window

Most tariffs give importers a short grace period — often the first two or three days — free of storage charges, to allow time for the paperwork, duty payment and customs examination that every shipment has to go through before it can be released. This is the single biggest lever importers have: if you can clear your shipment within the free window, you can avoid demurrage almost entirely.

NoteNot every cargo category gets a free window. General cargo typically does; dangerous goods and cold-chain cargo are usually billed from day one because of the extra handling and monitoring involved.

How the daily rate changes over time

Godown rent isn't a flat daily fee. Tariffs are structured in day-brackets — for example, days 1-3, days 3-5, days 6-10, and 11 days onward — and the rate per kilogram increases the longer cargo stays. This is a deliberate design: it discourages importers from treating the godown as long-term storage and encourages fast clearance.

Weight tiers matter too

On top of the day-bracket, the rate itself depends on how heavy the shipment is. Lighter shipments (up to 50 kg) are usually charged a flat rate for the whole consignment. Heavier shipments (above 50 kg) are charged per kilogram, per day — which is why demurrage on a large shipment can climb into the hundreds of thousands of rupees if it sits for a week or two.

What's included besides rent

  • D/O Fee — a delivery order fee charged by the handling agent to release the cargo.
  • Documentation charges — a fixed administrative fee per shipment.
  • Handling charges — for physically moving, weighing and storing the cargo, tiered by weight.
  • Sales tax — applied on the subtotal of the above, typically at 15%.

A worked example: the same 30 kg shipment on three different days

The quickest way to understand godown rent is to follow one shipment through time. Take a 30 kg consignment of general cargo. Because it is 50 kg or under, it is charged a flat amount for the whole consignment each day, not per kilogram. The first three days are free, so the clock for chargeable days starts on day 4.

Where do those rent figures come from? On day 6 the flat daily rate for light cargo is Rs. 1,839, and three days are chargeable (days 4, 5 and 6), so 1,839 × 3 = Rs. 5,517. On day 12 the rate has moved into the final bracket at Rs. 2,137, and nine days are chargeable, so 2,137 × 9 = Rs. 19,233. Notice what happened: the shipment stayed only six days longer than at day 6, yet the rent more than tripled. That is the combined effect of a higher bracket rate and more chargeable days.

Also notice that Rs. 15,323 of the day-3 bill has nothing to do with storage at all. The D/O fee, documentation and handling are paid on every shipment regardless of how fast it is cleared. Fast clearance removes the rent, but never the fixed charges.

Reading your own bill: a short checklist

  1. Find the arrival date. Rent is counted from the day cargo is received into the godown, which is usually the flight date or the day after, not the day your documents are ready.
  2. Count free days first. If your category has a free window, subtract it before multiplying anything.
  3. Check the weight used. Charges apply to the weight recorded by the warehouse, which may be the gross or chargeable weight on the airway bill rather than your invoice weight.
  4. Match the bracket. Identify the day-bracket your gate-out day falls into and the correct weight tier.
  5. Add fixed charges and tax last. Sales tax is applied to the subtotal, not to rent alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is the arrival day counted as day 1?

In our calculator, arrival counts as day 1 and the gate-out date is the last day counted. If your operator counts differently, the total can shift by one day of rent, so always confirm the counting rule with the warehouse.

Why is my bill different from the estimate?

The most common reasons are a different recorded weight, an extra service such as forklift or console charges, a gate-out on a different date than planned, or a tariff revision after the rates were last updated here.

Do weekends and public holidays count as storage days?

Generally yes. Godown rent runs on calendar days, so a long weekend in the middle of your clearance can silently add chargeable days.

Can I pay rent only for part of a shipment?

If a consignment is cleared in partial deliveries, the remaining cargo continues to accrue rent. Ask the operator how partial releases are billed before agreeing to one.

Is godown rent the same at every airline shed?

No. Each operator publishes its own tariff. That is why this site has separate calculators for Gerry'sDnata, SAPS and PIA instead of a single generic one.
