Godown Rent on Import Shipments: The Complete Guide

What godown rent actually is, why it's charged, and how the free-storage window works.

When a shipment lands at an airport or port, it doesn't clear customs and leave the same hour. Between the flight landing and the importer's truck driving out with the goods, the cargo sits in a bonded warehouse — commonly called a godown — operated by the airline's ground handling agent or a private warehousing company. Godown rent is the fee charged for that storage space, and it's calculated per day, per kilogram, based on published tariff tables.

Why does godown rent exist?

Warehouse space at an airport is limited and expensive to maintain — it needs security, climate control in many cases, forklifts, staff, and insurance. Ground handling companies recover this cost by charging importers rent for every day their cargo occupies a slot, starting from the day it's offloaded from the aircraft.

The free-storage window

Most tariffs give importers a short grace period — often the first two or three days — free of storage charges, to allow time for the paperwork, duty payment and customs examination that every shipment has to go through before it can be released. This is the single biggest lever importers have: if you can clear your shipment within the free window, you can avoid demurrage almost entirely.

NoteNot every cargo category gets a free window. General cargo typically does; dangerous goods and cold-chain cargo are usually billed from day one because of the extra handling and monitoring involved.

How the daily rate changes over time

Godown rent isn't a flat daily fee. Tariffs are structured in day-brackets — for example, days 1-3, days 3-5, days 6-10, and 11 days onward — and the rate per kilogram increases the longer cargo stays. This is a deliberate design: it discourages importers from treating the godown as long-term storage and encourages fast clearance.

Weight tiers matter too

On top of the day-bracket, the rate itself depends on how heavy the shipment is. Lighter shipments (up to 50 kg) are usually charged a flat rate for the whole consignment. Heavier shipments (above 50 kg) are charged per kilogram, per day — which is why demurrage on a large shipment can climb into the hundreds of thousands of rupees if it sits for a week or two.

What's included besides rent

  • D/O Fee — a delivery order fee charged by the handling agent to release the cargo.
  • Documentation charges — a fixed administrative fee per shipment.
  • Handling charges — for physically moving, weighing and storing the cargo, tiered by weight.
  • Sales tax — applied on the subtotal of the above, typically at 15%.
