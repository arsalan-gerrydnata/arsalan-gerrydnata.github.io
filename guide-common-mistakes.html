Common Importer Mistakes That Inflate Your Warehouse Bill

Eight avoidable errors we see again and again, each with a number attached.

General information for planning purposes. Procedures, systems and fees are revised by the authorities and operators from time to time, so confirm current requirements with your clearing agent.

Most large demurrage bills are not caused by bad luck. They come from a small set of repeat mistakes. Each of them is preventable, and several take less than an hour to fix. To show the cost, the figures below use the general cargo tariff from our Gerry'sDnata calculator.

1. Starting the paperwork after the cargo arrives

If documents, permits and payment arrangements only begin when the cargo lands, the free window is gone before the declaration is even filed. A 30 kg general shipment cleared on day 3 costs Rs. 17,621 in total. Cleared on day 6 it costs Rs. 23,966. The extra Rs. 6,345 buys nothing.

2. Not knowing which category applies

General cargo has three free days; dangerous goods and cold storage have none. Importers who assume every shipment gets free days are surprised when a 40 kg DG consignment cleared in two days still carries Rs. 5,014 in rent. Always declare the right category and check its terms before you ship.

3. Ignoring the weight bands

Handling and rent change at 50 kg, 100 kg and 200 kg. At 50 kg of general cargo the handling is Rs. 4,269; at 51 kg it is Rs. 5,431 and rent switches from a flat rate to a per-kilogram rate. If the airway bill shows a weight just over a band because of packing, ask whether lighter packaging or a corrected weight is possible before you ship.

4. Forgetting that weekends count

A shipment released on Friday evening and collected on Monday has paid for two extra days. For a 120 kg general shipment in the second bracket, each extra day can cost about Rs. 7,440 before tax.

5. Choosing an agent on price alone

A cheap agent who files late, answers slowly or misses an examination call costs far more than a good agent's higher fee. Ask how quickly declarations are filed and who handles examination.

6. Leaving optional charges unchecked

Some operators list optional items such as console charges (SAPS Rs. 5,860, PIA Rs. 7,000) and delivery order fees that apply only to certain shipments. Review each line of the operator's bill and ask why it is there.

7. Not keeping records of delays you did not cause

If a system outage or the operator's own mistake delays you, a written record is your only basis for a waiver. Screenshots with timestamps and email confirmations make the case.

8. Letting heavy cargo sit

For heavy cargo the cost of delay is brutal. A 500 kg general shipment cleared on day 4 totals Rs. 66,187; on day 10 it is Rs. 297,912. For anything above a few hundred kilograms, every morning matters.

A quick pre-shipment checklist

  • Invoice, packing list and AWB checked against each other
  • Permits and licences confirmed before dispatch
  • Correct cargo category declared
  • Duty and operator payments funded
  • Collection vehicle planned for the first free day
  • Estimate prepared with the calculator for your expected clearance date

Frequently asked questions

Which mistake is the most expensive?

Delay on heavy cargo. Because rent is per kilogram per day, large consignments multiply every lost day.

Can I claim back demurrage caused by an operator error?

You can request a waiver, but it is at the operator's discretion. Written evidence improves your chances.

Is it better to pay a premium agent?

Not always, but speed, responsiveness and clear communication are worth paying for.

How early should I start preparing?

Before the goods are dispatched. Permits and approvals can take longer than the flight.

Should I check the estimate against the real bill?

Yes. Compare the operator's bill line by line with the estimate and ask about any difference.
