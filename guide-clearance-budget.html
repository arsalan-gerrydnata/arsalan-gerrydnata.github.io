How to Budget the Full Cost of an Air Import

A simple template that covers every line, so no charge surprises you.

Last updated: 11 November 2026 · General information for planning; procedures and fees can change, so confirm with your clearing agent and operator.

Many importers budget for the goods and the freight, then get surprised by the charges at the airport. A short, honest budget prepared before you ship avoids that. Here is a template you can copy into a spreadsheet.

The budget template

Worked example: the warehouse part for 80 kg of general cargo

The D/O fee (Rs. 9,828) and documentation charges (Rs. 1,226) are included in the subtotals. The difference between the two scenarios is Rs. 5,704, which is your delay buffer for a two-day slip.

Build a contingency in days, not percentages

Instead of adding a vague 10%, ask what a two-day or a five-day delay would cost, and add that figure. For heavy cargo the contingency should be larger, because rent is charged per kilogram. Our calculator lets you test this by changing only the gate-out date.

Review after each shipment

After every import, compare your budget with the real cost line by line. Keep the notes. After three or four shipments you will know exactly where your estimates are off, and your next budget will be much tighter.

Frequently asked questions

Which cost is the least predictable?

Examination-related delays, because they extend storage time and add handling and labour.

Should I include transport?

Yes. Late or unavailable trucks are a common cause of extra storage days.

Do the calculators include customs duties?

No. They estimate only the warehouse operator's charges.

How much contingency is sensible?

Enough to cover a two to three day slip at your cargo's weight and category.

Where can I check operator charges?

Use the calculator for your shed: Gerry'sDnata, SAPS or PIA.
