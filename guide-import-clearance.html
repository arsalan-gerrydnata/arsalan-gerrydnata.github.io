Every Charge on an Import Cargo Bill, Explained

D/O fee, documentation, handling, godown rent, sales tax — what each one actually pays for.

Import clearance invoices from a ground handling agent or warehouse can look intimidating — a stack of line items with no explanation. In reality, almost every import shipment is billed against the same handful of charge categories. Here's what each one covers.

1. D/O Fee (Delivery Order Fee)

This is a fixed fee charged by the airline's ground handling agent for issuing the delivery order — the document that authorises the warehouse to release your cargo to your clearing agent or transporter. It's charged once per shipment, regardless of weight or how long the cargo stayed.

2. Documentation charges

A flat administrative fee covering the paperwork processing associated with your shipment — manifest matching, record-keeping, and the various forms that move alongside the cargo.

3. Handling charges

This covers the physical work: unloading the cargo from the aircraft's unit load device or pallet, weighing it, moving it into the godown, and preparing it for eventual release. Handling charges are tiered by weight — heavier shipments cost more to physically move, so the fee scales with declared weight, usually in weight bands (e.g. up to 50 kg, 51–100 kg, 101–200 kg, and a per-kg rate beyond that).

4. Godown rent / demurrage

The storage fee for the time your cargo spends in the warehouse, calculated by day-bracket and weight as covered in our demurrage guide. This is usually the largest and most variable line item on the bill — and the one most within an importer's control.

5. Sales tax

A percentage-based tax (commonly 15%) applied to the subtotal of the charges above. Because it's a percentage, keeping the underlying charges — especially demurrage — low also reduces the tax owed on top of them.

Putting it together

A typical bill is structured as:

D/O Fee + Documentation + Handling + Godown Rent/Demurrage = Subtotal
Subtotal + Sales Tax (15%) = Total Payable
