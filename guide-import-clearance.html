Every Charge on an Import Cargo Bill, Explained

D/O fee, documentation, handling, godown rent, sales tax — what each one actually pays for.

Import clearance invoices from a ground handling agent or warehouse can look intimidating — a stack of line items with no explanation. In reality, almost every import shipment is billed against the same handful of charge categories. Here's what each one covers.

1. D/O Fee (Delivery Order Fee)

This is a fixed fee charged by the airline's ground handling agent for issuing the delivery order — the document that authorises the warehouse to release your cargo to your clearing agent or transporter. It's charged once per shipment, regardless of weight or how long the cargo stayed.

2. Documentation charges

A flat administrative fee covering the paperwork processing associated with your shipment — manifest matching, record-keeping, and the various forms that move alongside the cargo.

3. Handling charges

This covers the physical work: unloading the cargo from the aircraft's unit load device or pallet, weighing it, moving it into the godown, and preparing it for eventual release. Handling charges are tiered by weight — heavier shipments cost more to physically move, so the fee scales with declared weight, usually in weight bands (e.g. up to 50 kg, 51–100 kg, 101–200 kg, and a per-kg rate beyond that).

4. Godown rent / demurrage

The storage fee for the time your cargo spends in the warehouse, calculated by day-bracket and weight as covered in our demurrage guide. This is usually the largest and most variable line item on the bill — and the one most within an importer's control.

5. Sales tax

A percentage-based tax (commonly 15%) applied to the subtotal of the charges above. Because it's a percentage, keeping the underlying charges — especially demurrage — low also reduces the tax owed on top of them.

Putting it together

A typical bill is structured as:

D/O Fee + Documentation + Handling + Godown Rent/Demurrage = Subtotal
Subtotal + Sales Tax (15%) = Total Payable

The complete charge sheet, line by line

An import file collects several separate charges before your truck can leave the airport. Understanding which are fixed and which depend on time or weight is the key to budgeting properly.

Separately from the operator's bill, you will also pay customs duties and taxes through the customs system, the clearing agent's fee, transport, and any examination or lab charges. This site only estimates the warehouse operator's bill, because that is the part that changes with every day of delay.

A full example: 100 kg of general cargo cleared on day 4

  1. D/O fee: Rs. 9,828
  2. Documentation: Rs. 1,226
  3. Handling (51–100 kg band): Rs. 5,431
  4. Rent: day 4 means one chargeable day at Rs. 31 per kg, so 31 × 1 × 100 = Rs. 3,100
  5. Subtotal: Rs. 19,585
  6. Sales tax at 15%: Rs. 2,938
  7. Total: Rs. 22,523

Compare that with clearing on day 3: the rent drops to zero, the subtotal is Rs. 16,485, tax is Rs. 2,473 and the total is Rs. 18,958. Clearing one day later added Rs. 3,565 to the bill. The pattern matters more than the last rupee: about Rs. 16,500 of this bill is unavoidable, and everything above it is controlled by your speed.

Different operators, different fixed charges

Fixed charges are not identical across sheds. In our calculators, Gerry'sDnata charges a D/O fee of Rs. 9,828 and documentation of Rs. 1,226. SAPS lists a D/O fee of Rs. 8,490, a console charge of Rs. 5,860 and documentation of Rs. 842. PIA lists a D/O fee of Rs. 9,000, a console charge of Rs. 7,000 and documentation (MAWB) of Rs. 950. Console and D/O charges are optional in some cases, for example when the cargo is not a consolidated shipment, so ticking the right options matters.

Frequently asked questions

Which charges can I avoid?

Fixed fees cannot be avoided, but demurrage can. Optional lines such as console charges apply only when the shipment qualifies, so check whether they really belong on your file.

Who issues the delivery order?

The airline or its handling agent issues the D/O against payment of its charges and your original or surrendered airway bill.

Is the clearing agent fee included in these estimates?

No. The calculators estimate the warehouse operator's charges only. Duties, taxes and your agent's professional fee are separate.

Why do two shipments with the same weight pay different handling?

Handling rates differ by cargo category. Dangerous goods and temperature-controlled cargo have higher handling tiers than general cargo.

Do I pay tax on the tax?

No. Sales tax is calculated once, on the subtotal of the operator's charges.
