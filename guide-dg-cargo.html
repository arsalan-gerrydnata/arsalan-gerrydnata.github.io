Dangerous Goods (DG) Cargo Storage: What to Expect

Why DG cargo carries a higher tariff, and what "dangerous goods" actually covers.

"Dangerous Goods" is an aviation classification, not a judgment about how hazardous something looks. It covers a wide range of everyday commercial items — lithium batteries, aerosols, certain chemicals, magnetized materials, flammable liquids, and more — anything that falls under the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations for air transport.

Why DG storage costs more

DG cargo has to be stored in a separated, specially designated area of the warehouse, often with fire suppression, spacing requirements between different hazard classes, and stricter handling protocols. Staff handling DG cargo typically need specific certification. All of this raises the operating cost of the storage space, which is reflected in a higher tariff than general cargo.

No free storage window

Like cold storage cargo, DG cargo is generally billed from day one with no grace period — the warehouse is dedicating a compliant, segregated storage slot to it immediately on arrival.

Handling charges are higher too

Beyond the day-to-day storage rent, the handling charge — the one-time fee for moving and processing the shipment — is also set higher for DG cargo than general cargo, reflecting the extra care, documentation checks (like the Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods) and segregation involved in physically handling it.

Documentation matters more for DGDG shipments typically require additional paperwork — correct UN numbers, packing group, and hazard class declarations — before customs will even begin processing release. Missing or incorrect DG documentation is one of the most common reasons DG shipments end up sitting in storage far longer than intended.

Budgeting for a DG shipment

Because there's no free window and both handling and storage rates run higher, importers moving DG cargo should budget for a noticeably higher total than an equivalent-weight general cargo shipment — and should treat fast clearance as even more of a priority.

What counts as dangerous goods?

Dangerous goods are articles or substances that can endanger health, safety, property or the aircraft in flight. International rules group them into nine classes: explosives; gases; flammable liquids; flammable solids; oxidisers and organic peroxides; toxic and infectious substances; radioactive material; corrosives; and miscellaneous dangerous goods such as lithium batteries and magnetised material. Even items that look ordinary, such as perfumes, aerosols, e-cigarettes, power banks or some spare parts, can fall into these classes.

Why DG storage is billed differently

DG cargo must be stored in separate, segregated areas, handled by trained staff and documented more carefully. There is also no free window: charges start on day one. In the Gerry'sDnata DG tariff, handling for cargo up to 50 kg is Rs. 4,568, compared with Rs. 4,269 for general cargo, and the daily rent for light cargo starts at Rs. 2,507.

Worked example 1: 40 kg of DG cargo cleared in two days

Handling Rs. 4,568, rent Rs. 2,507 × 2 = Rs. 5,014, D/O Rs. 9,828 and documentation Rs. 1,226 give a subtotal of Rs. 20,636. Add Rs. 3,095 sales tax for a total of Rs. 23,731. The same weight of general cargo cleared on the same day would have paid no rent at all.

Radioactive and detained cargo

Two special categories also appear in the calculator. Radioactive material is billed on its own bracket (days 1–2, then day 3 onwards), at a flat rate up to 50 kg or per kilogram above that. Cargo detained by customs uses a three-bracket structure (up to 7 days, 8–15 days, 16 days and above). The handling charges for both are not published in the source tariff, so the calculator shows them as "to be confirmed" instead of inventing a number.

Paperwork that speeds DG clearance

  • A correct Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods matching the airway bill.
  • Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS/SDS) for the exact product.
  • Any import licence or no-objection certificate the regulator requires for that product class.
  • Packaging and marking that match what was declared on the airway bill.

Frequently asked questions

Do lithium batteries count as DG?

Yes, in most forms. Loose batteries and batteries packed with or contained in equipment are regulated, although the exact class and packing instruction depend on the type and size.

Why does a small DG parcel still cost so much?

Fixed charges and the high minimum handling and rent rates apply regardless of size, and there is no free storage window.

Can I reduce DG demurrage?

Yes, by completing licences and declarations in advance and arranging collection transport for the release day.

Is the DG tariff the same at every shed?

No. Each operator publishes its own DG rates. Use the calculator for your specific shed.

Where do radioactive items fit?

They are a DG class and use their own bracket structure in the Gerry'sDnata calculator.
