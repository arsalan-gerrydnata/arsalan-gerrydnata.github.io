How to Reduce Your Demurrage Bill

Practical steps experienced importers use to keep storage days — and charges — as low as possible.

Demurrage is one of the few import costs that's largely within your control — it's driven by time, and time can be managed. Here's what consistently makes the biggest difference.

1. Prepare documents before the shipment lands

The clock on storage charges starts the moment cargo is offloaded — not when your paperwork is ready. Have your invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and any permits or NOCs ready and reviewed before the flight even arrives, so clearance can start immediately.

2. Track your shipment's ETA closely

Knowing the exact arrival date lets you time your clearing agent's work and your transporter's pickup to land inside the free-storage window (where one applies) rather than finding out on day 4 that you've already missed it.

3. Pre-clear duty payment where possible

Delays in duty assessment or payment are one of the most common reasons cargo sits past the free window. Where your customs system allows advance or provisional assessment, use it.

4. Line up your transporter in advance

Cargo that's fully cleared by customs but has no truck booked to collect it still accrues godown rent every day it waits. Book transport before clearance finishes, not after.

5. Know your bracket boundaries

Because demurrage rates jump at day-bracket boundaries (see our demurrage guide), it's often worth pushing hard to clear cargo the day before a bracket changes rather than the day after — the cost difference can be disproportionate to the one extra day.

6. For DG and cold storage cargo, treat every day as billable

Since these categories usually have no free window, there's no early "buffer" to rely on. Every day of delay is a direct cost from day one — which makes advance documentation even more valuable for these shipment types.

A timeline that works for most importers

  1. Before the flight departs: collect the invoice, packing list and airway bill copy. Agree who will file the goods declaration and who will pay duties.
  2. When the flight is confirmed: pre-file the declaration where the system allows it, and confirm that your import registration and any permits are valid.
  3. Day of arrival: confirm that the cargo is received into the godown, obtain the delivery-order requirements, and check the recorded weight.
  4. Days 1–2: complete assessment and duty payment. Request examination early if your cargo is selected.
  5. Day 3 (last free day, if applicable): collect the delivery order, pay the operator's charges and arrange the gate pass and vehicle.

Five habits that save the most money

  • Estimate before you ship. Use the calculator with your expected weight and a realistic clearance day. A budget made in advance prevents surprises.
  • Keep a clearing agent who answers quickly. A slow agent is the most common cause of unnecessary storage days.
  • Do not let a shipment wait for payment. Arrange duty and operator payments in advance so cargo is never held for funds.
  • Check weight on the airway bill. An inflated weight raises both handling and per-kilogram rent.
  • Pick up on the same day. Do not leave a released shipment overnight. Every night is another day of rent.

When delay is not your fault

Sometimes cargo is held for reasons outside the importer's control, such as a customs system outage, a delayed manifest or an operator error. Keep timestamps, screenshots and emails. A written record is what makes a waiver request credible.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single most effective way to cut demurrage?

Clear inside the free window. For general cargo that means finishing the process by day 3.

Should I split a shipment to save money?

Rarely. Handling and fixed charges apply per shipment, so splitting usually adds fees without lowering rent.

Is it worth paying the operator before customs clears the goods?

Paying the operator's bill early does not stop storage rent, because rent runs until the cargo leaves the godown. Prepare payment in advance so you can act the minute release is granted.

Can a clearing agent be blamed for demurrage?

Check your agreement. Many agents are responsible for delays they cause, but not for delays at customs or the operator.
