The Airway Bill Explained: MAWB, HAWB and the Fields That Matter

The single document that identifies your cargo from departure to delivery.

Last updated: 11 November 2026 · General information for planning; procedures and fees can change, so confirm with your clearing agent and operator.

The airway bill (AWB) is the central document in air freight. It is a contract of carriage between the shipper and the airline, a receipt for the goods and the reference number used to track and release your cargo. Learning to read one takes ten minutes and can prevent expensive errors.

The AWB number

An air waybill number has 11 digits: a three-digit airline prefix, a seven-digit serial number and a check digit. The prefix tells you which airline issued it, and the number is what you use to track the shipment and what the warehouse uses to locate it.

Master versus house airway bill

Master AWB (MAWB)House AWB (HAWB)
Issued byThe airlineThe freight forwarder or consolidator
BetweenAirline and the forwarderForwarder and the actual shipper/consignee
Used whenCargo is moved in bulkSeveral customers' cargo is consolidated under one MAWB
Your document?Only if you ship directlyYes, if you use a forwarder who consolidates

When a forwarder combines many small shipments into one, the airline sees a single master airway bill, and each customer gets a house airway bill. This is why some operators charge extra console fees: the consolidated cargo needs to be broken down and separately released. In our SAPS and PIA calculators the console charge is therefore an optional tick box.

Fields to check before the flight arrives

  • Shipper and consignee names and addresses. They must match your importer registration exactly.
  • Airport of departure and destination and the flight routing.
  • Number of pieces, gross weight and chargeable weight. The warehouse often uses these figures for charges; see our Chargeable Weight Explained.
  • Nature and quantity of goods, which should match the invoice.
  • Freight charges: prepaid or collect. Collect cargo requires payment of freight at destination, which can slow release if you are not ready.
  • Handling information such as special codes for perishable, dangerous or valuable cargo.

Why one wrong digit hurts

If the consignee name differs even slightly from the name on your declaration, the cargo may not be released until an amendment is made by the airline or forwarder, which usually takes time and sometimes costs a fee. At the same time, rent keeps accumulating. For 120 kg of general cargo, each day after the free window costs about Rs. 3,720 to Rs. 7,440 before tax, depending on the bracket.

Three common situations

1. The consignee name is slightly different

"ABC Traders" on the airway bill but "ABC Traders (Pvt) Ltd" on your declaration. Ask the airline or forwarder for an amendment as soon as you notice it, and check whether an amendment fee applies.

2. The shipment is freight collect

You must pay the airline's freight at destination before the cargo is released. Make sure the amount is known and the funds are ready on arrival day.

3. The weight on the bill is higher than expected

If the airway bill shows a chargeable weight of 80 kg for cartons that weigh 30 kg on the scale, check the dimensions. It is probably volumetric weight, as explained in our chargeable weight guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is an e-AWB?

An electronic airway bill replaces the paper version with electronic data, with the same legal function. Not every route and carrier uses it.

Is the AWB a document of title?

No. Unlike a sea bill of lading, an airway bill is not negotiable and does not transfer ownership by itself.

How do I track my cargo?

Use the AWB number on the airline cargo website or the forwarder's tracking tool.

What does "collect" mean?

The consignee pays freight at destination, before the cargo is released.

Who can amend an airway bill?

Usually the issuing airline or forwarder, on written request from the shipper.
