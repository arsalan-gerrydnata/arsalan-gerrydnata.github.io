Damaged, Short or Missing Cargo: How to Make a Claim

What to do at the warehouse, which documents to keep and how long you have.

Last updated: 11 November 2026 · General information for planning; procedures and fees can change, so confirm with your clearing agent and operator.

Most air cargo arrives intact, but when it does not, the way you act in the first hours decides whether a claim succeeds. This article explains the practical steps and the typical time limits set by international air carriage rules. It is general information, not legal advice. Your airway bill and the applicable convention will set the exact rights.

Step 1: inspect before you accept

When you collect the cargo, inspect the packages for visible damage, wet or crushed cartons, broken seals or tape and weight differences. If you see a problem, do not simply sign for the cargo as received in good condition. Ask the warehouse to record the condition on the receipt or to issue a damage or discrepancy report.

Step 2: document everything

  • Photograph the packages from several sides, with the airway bill number visible.
  • Photograph the damaged contents separately.
  • Note the arrival date, time and who attended.
  • Keep the airway bill, invoice, packing list and delivery documents.
  • If the goods are temperature-sensitive, save any data logger readings.

Step 3: give written notice in time

Under the widely adopted Montreal Convention, the usual notice periods for international air cargo are 14 days from receipt for damage and 21 days from the date the cargo was placed at your disposal for delay. Claims must also be brought within two years of arrival or of the date the cargo should have arrived. For hidden damage discovered later, notify the carrier at once, as soon as you find it, and well inside the notice period. Check the airway bill conditions and ask your agent or a lawyer to confirm the periods that apply to your shipment.

Limits and insurance

Carrier liability for cargo is limited by weight under the convention unless a higher value was declared in advance, so the amount recoverable from the airline may be well below the value of the goods. This is why cargo insurance matters, especially for high-value shipments. Ask your forwarder or insurer how a claim is filed under your policy.

Storage charges during a dispute

A claim does not stop the warehouse clock. If you leave damaged cargo in the godown while the dispute continues, rent keeps accruing. For 100 kg of general cargo, each chargeable day at the second-bracket rate costs about Rs. 6,200 in rent alone. It is usually better to accept the cargo with a written remark, collect it and pursue the claim separately.

Sample wording for a written notice

Keep it short, factual and dated: "Airway bill no. ___ , flight ___ , arrived on ___ . On inspection at the warehouse on ___ we found ___ (describe damage or shortage). We hold the carrier responsible and request a damage report and a claim form. Photographs are attached." Send it by email and keep the delivery confirmation.

Mistakes that weaken a claim

  • Signing for the cargo as received in good condition, then complaining later.
  • Throwing away damaged packaging before an inspection.
  • Waiting until the notice period has almost ended.
  • Sending only verbal notice with no written record.

Frequently asked questions

Who do I claim against?

Normally the airline that issued or carried under the airway bill. Your forwarder can advise.

What if the box looks fine but the contents are broken?

Report it immediately in writing as concealed damage and keep the packaging for inspection.

Does the warehouse operator pay claims?

Operators have their own liability terms. The responsible party depends on where the damage occurred.

Do I need insurance if the airline is liable?

Airline liability is limited, so insurance is strongly advised for valuable cargo.

What if cargo is completely missing?

Ask the airline for a tracing request and give written notice at once.
