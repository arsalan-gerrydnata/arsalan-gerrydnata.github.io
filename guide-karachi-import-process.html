Air Cargo Import Clearance: The Step-by-Step Process

Follow a shipment from the moment the aircraft lands to the moment your truck leaves the gate.

General information for planning purposes. Procedures, systems and fees are revised by the authorities and operators from time to time, so confirm current requirements with your clearing agent.

Importers who understand the sequence of steps can plan their time, and demurrage depends almost entirely on time. This guide walks through the typical journey of an air shipment into Pakistan and explains where delays usually happen and where the warehouse clock is running.

Step 1: Before the flight lands

Your supplier issues the commercial invoice, packing list and airway bill (AWB). Your clearing agent needs copies of these to prepare the goods declaration. At this stage you should also check whether your product needs a permit, licence, laboratory test or no-objection certificate from a regulator, because these can take longer than the flight itself. The storage clock has not started yet, which makes this the cheapest time to fix problems.

Step 2: Arrival and manifest

The airline files its import general manifest (IGM) listing everything on the flight. After landing, cargo is offloaded and moved into the bonded warehouse run by the airline's ground handler or a cargo terminal operator. From this point it is recorded against your AWB, weighed, and the godown rent clock begins, including any free days your category allows.

Step 3: Filing the goods declaration

Your clearing agent files a goods declaration (GD) electronically in the customs clearance system, stating the description, value, quantity, tariff classification and country of origin. Accuracy matters: an incorrect classification or value is a common reason for a shipment being routed to examination or for a query from customs.

Step 4: Assessment and duty payment

Customs assesses the declaration and calculates duties and taxes. After assessment you pay these through the authorised banking channel. Keep the payment proof. Payment is a separate matter from the operator's warehouse bill, which is estimated by the calculators on this site.

Step 5: Examination, if selected

Some consignments are selected for physical examination. Cargo is opened and compared with the documents in the presence of your agent. This is the biggest source of unpredictable delay, and it is also where every godown day costs money. Well-prepared documents and a correct declaration reduce the chance of problems.

Step 6: Delivery order and operator charges

The airline or its handler issues a delivery order (D/O) after you present the required AWB documents and pay the operator's charges: D/O fee, documentation, handling and any godown rent. This is the bill our calculators estimate, and it is easier to budget if you know how many days will pass before you reach this step.

Step 7: Out-of-charge, gate pass and collection

When customs clears the goods and the operator has been paid, a gate pass is generated and your vehicle can enter to load the cargo. Remember that rent continues until the cargo physically leaves, so have your truck, loaders and driver ready.

Frequently asked questions

How long does import clearance take?

There is no fixed time. A clean consignment with correct documents can be cleared in a day or two, while an examined or queried shipment can take considerably longer. Plan for the free storage window and treat anything beyond it as a cost.

Do I need a clearing agent?

Importers can file themselves if registered, but most use a licensed customs agent because declarations, classification and follow-up with customs are specialised work.

What is an IGM?

An import general manifest is the carrier's official list of cargo on a flight. Your goods declaration is matched against it.

Who pays the warehouse operator?

The importer, normally through the clearing agent, before the delivery order is released.

Why is the weight on the bill different from my invoice?

Warehouse charges use the weight recorded by the operator, which can be a chargeable or gross weight and may differ from the net weight on your invoice.
