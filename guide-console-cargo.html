Console Cargo Explained: What Importers Should Know

Why shared shipments carry extra charges, and when you should pay them.

Last updated: 11 November 2026 · General information for planning; procedures and fees can change, so confirm with your clearing agent and operator.

If you import small shipments through a freight forwarder, your goods may travel as part of a consolidation. The term "console" appears on many warehouse bills, and importers often ask whether they should be paying it. This article explains what it means and how to check your own bill.

What is consolidated cargo?

A forwarder combines the cargo of several customers into one larger shipment and books it with the airline under a single master airway bill. Each customer receives a house airway bill covering their own goods. Consolidation lowers freight cost per kilogram, because airlines give better rates for larger volumes.

Why a console charge exists

At the destination, a single master shipment must be broken down into the individual customers' lots. Staff must separate packages, check them against house airway bills and release them separately. Terminal operators therefore may charge a console or deconsolidation fee for this work.

On the SAPS and PIA pages these charges are tick boxes because they do not apply to every shipment. A direct consignment with its own master airway bill may not need a console charge, while a house shipment from a consolidator typically does.

Checking your own bill

  1. Look at your airway bill number. If you hold a house AWB, your goods are part of a consolidation.
  2. Ask your forwarder whether the console charge is already included in their invoice to avoid paying twice.
  3. Compare each line of the operator's bill with the published tariff.
  4. Ask for an explanation of any line you cannot identify.

The cost of waiting for the rest of the lot

With consolidated cargo, release is sometimes slowed because the master shipment must be broken down first. Ask your forwarder for the expected release date, and plan your own paperwork so you can act the moment your lot is ready. Storage rent runs from arrival, not from the day your lot is separated, so delays are expensive. For example, a 60 kg lot of general cargo cleared on day 5 pays Rs. 3,720 in rent in our Gerry'sDnata calculator, but cleared on day 6 it pays Rs. 11,160, because the daily rate doubles at day 6.

Questions to ask your forwarder

  1. Is my cargo on a house or a master airway bill?
  2. Will the operator's console charge apply, and is it already in your invoice?
  3. When do you expect the master shipment to be broken down?
  4. Will you inform me the moment my lot is ready for the delivery order?
  5. Who pays if storage rent accrues because of deconsolidation delay?

Frequently asked questions

Is a console charge mandatory?

Only where the operator's tariff applies it to your type of shipment. Check the tariff and your airway bill type.

What if my forwarder also charges a handling fee?

Ask whether it is a different service or a duplicate of the operator's charge.

Does consolidation make clearance slower?

It can, because the master shipment has to be deconsolidated, so ask for the expected timeline.

Is consolidated cargo cheaper?

Often yes for freight, but the destination charges may be higher. Compare the total cost.

Where can I see the console charge for my shed?

On the SAPS and PIA calculator pages, as optional tick boxes.
